## 2. White space: is "evidence-gated autonomous product operations" a real category?

**Verdict: a real *property*, not a real *category* — and the property is commoditizing fast.**

1. **The design idea has been independently converged on and is now in the literature.** Separating a build plane from an assurance plane, a human-approved "Verification Contract", and a release gate only independent evidence can pass is the explicit subject of [arXiv 2610.28667 "Who Verifies the Builder?"](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28667v1), [arXiv 2608.13867](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13867) and [arXiv 2610.08900 "Humanize"](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08900v2) (VERIFIED). The last catalogues 72 deterministic mechanical gates, separates builder from reviewer by *vendor*, and requires "oracles outside both agents." A well-funded competitor can read the same three papers.
2. **Vendors already sell the components.** mabl ships pre-PR agent checks and argues authorship ≠ verification. Harness ships deployment verification with auto-rollback. Agentforce, Datadog, Harness, CrewAI AMP and Zapier Agents all sell governance, approvals, budgets and audit logs. Qodo, Sonar, Snyk and Semgrep sell deterministic gates — a *stronger* guarantee than UNTIL's in one respect, because a rule engine cannot be fooled by a hallucinating builder.
3. **The real white space is narrow and structural, not conceptual:** *no vendor ships a product loop where the owner defines "done" and the system itself cannot renegotiate that definition, persists across crashes, refuses late completions, produces a content-hashed release candidate, and closes the loop on an observed metric.* Every element exists. The **composition with a published, reproducible proof bundle** does not.

**Where UNTIL is far behind, stated plainly.** The competitor overlapping UNTIL's *execution* claim most directly is not Devin but **mabl + Qodo + Factory**, which between them cover independent-ish verification, org-enforced acceptance rules, and long-running autonomous execution. Against those, UNTIL today has **zero production deployments, zero customer integrations, zero connectors, no multi-tenancy, no hosted product, no SOC 2/ISO, one person, ~2 100 lines of core, and evidence limited to bounded JSON deliverables plus one repository regression**. It has **no SWE-bench-class number**, so it cannot be compared with Devin, Factory, OpenHands or mini-SWE-agent on any axis the market currently measures. The website replays recorded traces rather than calling models in-browser — a demo, and a sophisticated CTO will discount it accordingly.

**Honest framing.** UNTIL is not competing with Devin; it is competing with the *internal CI pipeline plus a bored senior engineer*. The category, if one exists, is "verified autonomous operations for regulated or reputation-sensitive delivery" — a **niche with a narrow buyer**, not a broad horizontal market. Note that the founder's ambition of "replacing a full product+engineering team" is the one thing this white space *cannot* serve: a whole team has no crisp, attributable, countable outcome, so it cannot be evidence-gated, cannot be outcome-priced, and cannot be proven.

## 4. Pricing strategy

**Recommendation: open-core (MIT core) + a hosted control plane billed per workspace, with an outcome-priced tier as the second rung. Do NOT bill per seat.**

Per-seat penalises the buyer for *removing* humans (the founder's stated goal of replacing a team) and loses against free Cursor/Claude seats. Per-outcome matches the proven 2026 pattern — Sierra, Fin ($0.99/outcome), Zendesk ($1.50–2.00/verified resolution) — but only works where the outcome is crisply attributable. A verified release candidate **is**; "a team replaced" is not.

**Competitive price band in RUB** (verified list prices, Nov 2026): individual coding agents **657–16 430 ₽/mo**; team coding seats **2 465–3 286 ₽/seat**; a 30-dev team on Cursor Teams ≈ **74 000 ₽/mo**; workflow platforms **1 841–13 062 ₽/mo**; code review **1 643–5 915 ₽/dev/mo**; QA platforms **41 076 ₽/mo** (Momentic Scale) or quote (mabl); outcome-priced agents ≈ **$1–2.5 per resolution** (REPORTED), Agentforce **8.2 ₽/action**.

| **1. MIT self-hosted** | **0 ₽** | Already exists and is the credibility engine: zero runtime deps, zero telemetry, no account, no key. The published proof bundle *is* the marketing. Do not add a paid "community" tier that weakens the signal |

## 5. The single most defensible wedge

**The offline-verifiable acceptance ledger, running entirely inside the buyer's perimeter, against a Russian-resident model — sold as "the only agent product your security reviewer can approve."**

The artifact: a **hash-chained, content-addressed evidence bundle** — goal → evidence ledger → contract → independent check results → release-candidate hash → outcome observation — running with zero runtime dependencies, zero telemetry and zero network calls against a local model, replayable **offline** by a CISO or ISO auditor to answer "what did the system decide, on what evidence, and who could have overridden it?"

**Why a well-funded competitor cannot copy this in six months:**
1. **Negative-EV market for them.** Russian localisation, Roskomnadzor notification, processor agreements, RU-language acceptance semantics and GigaChat/YandexGPT-class local inference are a compliance-shaped product, not a feature. For a US vendor it is a market they have no reason to enter.
2. **It breaks their SaaS economics.** Local-first, zero-dependency, offline-replayable means no hosted control plane, no seat telemetry, no per-token margin. Every competitor in §1 monetises exactly what UNTIL refuses to collect.
3. **It is an integration-and-proof product, not a model product.** The hard part is the *chain* — state → fencing → independent checks → hash-locked release → outcome → audit export — proven by a reproducible test suite. Six months of a funded team's time suffices to copy the chain; it does not suffice to produce a chain that survives an auditor's scrutiny, which requires the *proof artifacts*, not the code.
4. **Distribution is local.** Russian OSS credibility, Russian-language documentation and hh.ru reach are assets a global competitor cannot rent.

**The one thing that can invalidate this wedge:** if buyers turn out to purchase *throughput* (more releases per developer-month) rather than *provability*, then Cursor/Devin/Factory win on price and capability and UNTIL loses regardless of proof quality. That is the single question the next three customer conversations must answer.
